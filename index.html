What's on the Ballot?

On June 9, Easthampton voters will decide whether to approve a Prop 2.5 override to fund essential city services. Massachusetts law limits property tax growth to 2.5% per year — this override is a permanent voter-approved measure to close the gap between that limit and what it actually costs to run our city.

Why Does Easthampton Need an Override?

Massachusetts Proposition 2½

Proposition 2½ is a state law that limits how much a city's property tax levy can grow each year — to 2.5% plus new growth from new construction. This law was passed in 1980 to protect homeowners from runaway tax increases.

But the cost of providing city services doesn't follow that same limit. Inflation, healthcare premiums for city employees, special education mandates, and the cost of maintaining aging infrastructure have all grown faster than 2.5% per year. Over time, this creates a structural gap — and eventually, services have to be cut.

This Is Not About Mismanagement

The City of Easthampton has worked hard to close this gap through operational efficiencies, shared services, and competitive grants. But there is a limit to what savings can accomplish. An override is the only mechanism available under Massachusetts law to address a structural revenue gap.

Not an Expansion — A Lifeline

This override does not fund new programs or city expansion. It funds the services Easthampton already has — schools, fire, police, library, senior services — that residents depend on every day. These are not extras. They are the foundation of what makes Easthampton a great place to live.
