The Big Picture

Why the 2.5% Rule Is Broken

For over 20 years, Massachusetts towns have been squeezed between rising costs and a law that limits tax revenue growth to 2.5% a year. Inflation keeps winning. Towns keep losing ground.

Source: Massachusetts Municipal Association, "A Perfect Storm" (October 2025) →

What's happening, in plain English

Proposition 2½ says that towns in Massachusetts can only raise property taxes by 2.5% each year. That sounds reasonable — until you realize that the actual cost of running a town (payroll, utilities, healthcare) rises faster than 2.5% most years. This gap has been building for two decades.

When you spend money slower than costs are rising, you fall further behind every year. Over time, that gap becomes a crisis. Easthampton — along with 57 other Massachusetts towns right now — is hitting that wall. The city has to choose: either find new revenue with an override, or cut services deeply.

This isn't a spending problem. It's a math problem. And the only way to solve it is to acknowledge what's really happening: inflation, not mismanagement.

The State Invests in Itself, Towns Get Cut

Since 2010, the Massachusetts state government has grown spending by 2.8% per year. Cities and towns? Just 0.6%. Three times slower.

Massachusetts Gives Towns Less Help Than Other States

Across the U.S., state governments send about 31 cents of every local tax dollar. In Massachusetts, it's only 26 cents. We're below average.

What This Means for Easthampton

Easthampton isn't alone. Right now, 57 Massachusetts towns are facing the exact same gap. Marshfield ($7M deficit), Malden ($8M), Lexington ($4.7M). This isn't poor management — it's a statewide structural problem baked into how Massachusetts funds local government.

Easthampton's bond rating is AA+. The city entered this crisis in excellent financial shape. The gap opened up because inflation outpaced the 2.5% cap. That's not a reason to blame the city. It's a reason to understand that the system itself is broken — and that an override is the right, honest answer.

Sources: MMA October 2025 report; Fitch Ratings
